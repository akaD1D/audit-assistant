IFRS 15 — Revenue from Contracts with Customers
Source: Wikipedia (CC BY-SA), summary for reference/education.

## Overview
IFRS 15 is an International Financial Reporting Standard developed by the IASB with the FASB. It provides unified guidance on recognizing revenue from customer contracts. Adopted in 2014; effective January 2018.

## Development History
Emerged from a decade-long IASB/FASB convergence project begun in 2002 due to inconsistencies between the boards' revenue definitions. Discussion paper 2008; exposure drafts 2010 and 2011; final standard issued 28 May 2014.

## The Five-Step Revenue Model
1. Identify the contract — Both parties approve and commit to performance, with identifiable rights, obligations, and payment terms demonstrating "commercial substance." Simple retail transactions qualify.
2. Identify performance obligations — Goods/services are distinct performance obligations if usable independently and separately identifiable from other contract elements.
3. Determine transaction price — Generally stipulated in contracts. Variable consideration uses the most likely amount or expected value method. Non-cash consideration measured at fair value.
4. Allocate price to obligations — Transaction price distributed across identified performance obligations.
5. Recognize revenue upon fulfillment — Recognized when the customer receives all benefits associated with the performance obligation.

## Key Recognition Principles
Significant Financing Components: When payment timing provides substantial financing, transaction price adjusts using present/future value, with differences recognized as interest income or expense.
Performance Over Time: When obligations settle gradually, revenue follows input methods (manufactured goods) or output methods, measuring progress toward completion.
Contract Assets: Introduced for accrued revenue when customer payment is conditional beyond the passage of time — distinct from typical trade receivables.

## Implementation Impact
May cause revenue recognition earlier in some cases and later in others compared to previous guidance, depending on contract structures and industry practices.

## Effective Date
Effective for annual reporting periods beginning on or after 1 January 2018; early adoption permitted. FASB's parallel ASC 606 became effective for public entities after 15 December 2017.
