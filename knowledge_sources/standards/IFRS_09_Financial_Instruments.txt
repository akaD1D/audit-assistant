IFRS 9 — Financial Instruments
Source: Wikipedia (CC BY-SA), summary for reference/education.

## Objective and Scope
IFRS 9 addresses the accounting for financial instruments. Issued by the IASB, it replaced IAS 39 and became effective 1 January 2018. It covers three areas: classification and measurement, impairment of financial assets, and hedge accounting.

## Development History
Developed with the FASB after the 2008 financial crisis. IASB developed it in phases 2009–2014; final consolidated version released 24 July 2014.

## Classification and Measurement
Financial Assets — classification depends on two tests:
1. Contractual Cash Flow Test (SPPI): cash flows must be "solely payments of principal and interest" to qualify for amortized cost or FVOCI.
2. Business Model Assessment:
   - Amortized Cost: held to collect contractual cash flows.
   - FVOCI: held to collect and to sell.
   - FVPL: all others, including trading assets and most derivatives.
Financial Liabilities — mostly amortized cost, except under the fair value option, where changes due to the entity's own credit risk go to other comprehensive income.

## Impairment Model (Expected Credit Loss)
- 12-Month Initial Allowance: on acquisition, impairment equals expected credit losses for next 12 months.
- Significant Increase in Credit Risk (SICR): triggers lifetime loss measurement.
- Credit-impaired-on-acquisition assets: measured using full lifetime losses.
- Recognizes projected lifetime losses faster than the previous incurred-loss model. Contrasts with FASB's CECL (full lifetime recognition immediately).

## Hedge Accounting
Aligns accounting with risk management activities: expanded eligibility for non-financial entities, relaxed effectiveness testing, enhanced disclosures, more flexibility for hedging components and groups.

## Effective Date
Mandatory 1 January 2018 (transition from IAS 39).
