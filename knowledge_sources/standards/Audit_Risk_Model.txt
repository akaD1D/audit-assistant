Audit Risk and the Audit Risk Model (ISA 200)
Source: Wikipedia (CC BY-SA), summary for reference/education.

## Definition
Audit risk (residual risk) is the risk that the auditor expresses an inappropriate opinion when the financial statements are materially misstated.

## The Three Components
Inherent Risk (IR): susceptibility of an assertion to misstatement before considering controls, due to the nature of the business/transaction (e.g. cash transactions carry higher inherent risk than settlement by cheque).
Control Risk (CR): the risk that a misstatement is not prevented or detected by internal controls (e.g. weak segregation of duties increases CR).
Detection Risk (DR): the risk that the auditor's procedures fail to detect a material misstatement, from sampling or non-sampling error.

## The Audit Risk Model
AR = IR × CR × DR
Audit risk compounds when component risks are elevated; reducing any factor reduces total audit risk. Auditors set acceptable detection risk (and thus the extent of substantive testing) given assessed IR and CR to achieve a target audit risk.
