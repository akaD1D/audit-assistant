Materiality in Auditing (ISA 320)
Source: Wikipedia (CC BY-SA), summary for reference/education.

## Definition
Materiality concerns the importance/significance of an amount, transaction, or discrepancy. The auditor opines on whether financial statements are prepared, in all material respects, in conformity with the applicable framework (GAAP/IFRS).
IASB definition (effective 1 Jan 2020): "Information is material if omitting, misstating or obscuring it could reasonably be expected to influence the decisions that the primary users of general purpose financial statements make."

## How Materiality is Determined
ISA 320: assessing materiality is a matter of professional judgement; no uniform quantitative threshold is mandated, but benchmarks are permitted.

Common single-rule benchmarks:
- 5% of pre-tax income
- 0.5% of total assets
- 1% of equity
- 1% of total revenue

Discussion Paper 6 (1984) ranges:
- 0.5%–1% of gross revenue
- 1%–2% of total assets
- 5%–10% of net profit

Advanced methods use concave/"gauge" functions giving decreasing tolerance (lower %) for larger companies.

## Performance Materiality
ISA 320 requires performance materiality — an amount below overall materiality applied to particular transactions/accounts — to reduce the risk that aggregate uncorrected misstatements become material.

## Role Under ISA 320
Applied throughout planning and execution. Inverse relationship between audit risk and materiality: higher risk → lower materiality threshold. Materiality has quantitative (monetary) and qualitative dimensions (e.g. omitted disclosures can be material regardless of amount).
