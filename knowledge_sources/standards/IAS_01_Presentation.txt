IAS 1 — Presentation of Financial Statements
Source: Wikipedia (CC BY-SA), summary for reference/education.

## Overview and Objective
IAS 1 establishes guidelines for presenting general-purpose financial statements under IFRS. Purpose: to provide useful information on the financial position, financial performance, and cash flows of an entity.

## Complete Set of Financial Statements
Categorizes information into: assets and liabilities; income and expenses; owner contributions and distributions; cash flows. A complete set includes the statement of financial position and the statement of comprehensive income (plus changes in equity, cash flows, and notes).

## General Features
1. Fair presentation and IFRS compliance.
2. Going concern basis.
3. Accrual accounting.
4. Materiality — material classes of items presented separately.
5. No offsetting of assets and liabilities.
6. Frequency — at least annually.
7. Consistency and comparative (prior-period) information.

## Structure and Content
Specifies minimum line items and classification rules, including:
- Separate current vs non-current liabilities and assets.
- Clear current/non-current classification criteria.
- Comprehensive notes, disclosure of accounting policies and assumptions.

## History
Issued 1997 by the IASC; adopted by the IASB in 2001; significantly amended, most recently 2011.
